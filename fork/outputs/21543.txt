The black water breathes in slow, heavy waves,
swallowing the moon’s pale, fractured face.
Salt spray clings to the hull like frost,
cold and sharp against the wood.

Far from the harbor’s warm embrace,
the boat drifts in the void.
Oars dip silently,
breaking the surface tension,
leaving ripples that vanish instantly.

Above, the stars are scattered shards,
too distant to guide, too bright to trust.
Then, a beam cuts through the dark,
a golden spear piercing the night.

The lighthouse stands on the jagged rock,
unmoving, unyielding.
Its light is a promise,
a tether to the world of men.

The boat turns toward the glow,
seeking the edge of the known.
The sea roars, a low, endless hum,
but the light holds steady.

It is the only anchor,
the only truth in the deep.
The boat approaches,
small and fragile,
against the vast, indifferent dark.
